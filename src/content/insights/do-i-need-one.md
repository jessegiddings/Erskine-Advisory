We sell representation, so discount accordingly. But the honest answer is *not always* — and a firm that can't say when you don't need it shouldn't be trusted about when you do.

## When you likely don't

- **The project is modest and you are close to it.** A straightforward renovation with a builder you have direct experience of, on a site you visit weekly, with terms you understand — the overhead of formal representation may exceed its value. Have a construction lawyer read the contract before you sign; that alone puts you ahead of most owners.
- **You genuinely have the expertise and the time.** Some owners have run capital projects professionally. If you can read a schedule of values, price a change order, and — this is the real test — have the *hours per week* to do it for two years, you may be your own representative.
- **A trusted institution is already verifying.** Some lenders' draw processes are genuinely rigorous. They protect the loan rather than you, but on simple projects the overlap can be enough.

## When the answer is probably yes

- **The project is large relative to your attention, not just your wealth.** The relevant ratio is not budget to net worth; it is project complexity to the hours and expertise you can actually give it. A demanding career is precisely what makes self-representation fail.
- **You are far from the site.** Building in another city or country means every fact reaches you through someone with a position in it. Distance converts trust from a preference into your entire control system. (More on this: [Building From a Distance](/insights/building-from-a-distance).)
- **The site or delivery model is complex.** Difficult terrain, extensive engineering, factory-built components, multiple prime contracts — each adds interfaces, and interfaces are where risk hides.
- **The team is new to you.** First project with this architect, this builder, this region's practices. References are past tense; verification is present tense.
- **Something already feels wrong.** Trust your unease. If the reporting has gone vague or the site has gone quiet, you don't need a representative eventually — you need [an independent account of where the project stands](/services/project-recovery) now.

## The test in one question

Would you commit the same capital to any other venture — an acquisition, a fund, a partnership — on the strength of reporting produced solely by the people receiving the money? If no, the only real question is scale: full representation, or a scoped engagement at the point of maximum leverage — [before you sign](/services/pre-construction-audit).
