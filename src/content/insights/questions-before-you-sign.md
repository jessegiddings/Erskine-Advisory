An owner reads the price and the finish date. The rest of the document exists to decide what happens when either one moves — and the rest of the document was drafted by the side with more practice. These nine questions cost nothing before signatures and a great deal after. Take the answers in writing, and take them to a construction lawyer; this article is commercial context, not legal advice.

**1. What exactly is excluded?** The exclusions and qualifications pages are where a low bid lives. Rock removal, utility connections, permit fees, landscaping, appliance installation — every excluded item is a change order waiting, with no competitive pressure on its price.

**2. Which numbers are allowances — and whose contingency is it?** An allowance is a placeholder, and a placeholder set low makes the bid look good; ask what each one is based on and what comparable projects actually spent against it. The same scrutiny goes to contingency: a contingency inside the contract price that the builder spends without your approval is margin wearing a disguise. Who authorizes drawing on it, and where does the unspent balance go?

**3. How are change orders priced and approved?** Fixed markup on documented cost, or "to be agreed"? Who can order changes, and what happens to work performed without a signed change order? This clause decides more of your final cost than the bid does.

**4. What does the payment schedule actually track?** Payments should follow verifiable work in place, not the calendar. Front-loaded schedules transfer risk to you with every early draw — and ask how holdback or retainage works in your jurisdiction, because it is the leverage that gets deficiencies finished.

**5. What happens when the schedule slips?** Is the completion date enforceable, or aspirational? What counts as an excusable delay, what notice is required, and what — concretely — is the remedy when the date moves for reasons that aren't excusable?

**6. What insurance is actually in place?** Not "are you insured" but: certificates, limits, and who carries builder's risk. Confirm coverage directly with the broker, not the contractor.

**7. Who is actually doing the work?** Which trades are the GC's own forces and which are subcontracted — and can key subcontractors be swapped for cheaper ones after signing without your consent?

**8. What are the warranty terms, in writing?** Duration, what's covered, what voids it, and the process for claims — plus manufacturer warranties, which often outlast the builder's and require registration nobody remembers to do.

**9. What happens if you need to part ways?** Termination rights in both directions, and what you owe on exit. The time to understand the divorce clause is before the marriage; mid-project, its terms are your entire negotiating position.

## The pattern

Every question aims at the same thing: converting assurances into terms. A good builder answers all nine comfortably — and the comfort of the answers is itself information. If a question produces irritation instead of a clause reference, you have learned something cheaper now than later.
