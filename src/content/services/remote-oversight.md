## The engagement

A payment application is a claim: that percentages are complete, that materials are stored, that milestones are reached. On most private projects, no one independent ever checks that claim against the site before the money moves — the person certifying progress is paid by the party requesting payment. If you are building at a distance — another city, another country — you may not see the site between draws at all.

Remote oversight closes that gap. Every draw is reviewed against the schedule of values and verified against work in place before you release it, with site verification performed by retained independent inspectors in the project's region and reviewed centrally by the same people every time.

## What each cycle includes

- **Pay application review.** The application checked against the schedule of values, prior draws, and the contract's payment terms — including holdback and lien compliance for the project's jurisdiction.
- **Site verification.** An independent inspection of claimed progress: work in place, materials genuinely on site and properly stored, milestones actually reached.
- **A written recommendation.** Release, hold, or release-in-part, with reasons — delivered before the payment deadline, in time to act on.
- **The running picture.** Draw against budget, change orders against contingency, schedule against baseline, photographs of actual conditions — so distance stops meaning ignorance.

## Why it changes behaviour

The first verified draw usually recovers little. The discipline is cumulative: schedules of values get more honest, applications get more accurate, and conversations get shorter when everyone knows the claim will be checked. Verification is less a policing function than a change in what it is worth anyone's time to submit.
